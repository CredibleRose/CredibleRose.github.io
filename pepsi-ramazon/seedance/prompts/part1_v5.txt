Fast-cut 20-second Ramadan commercial of 13 short shots, filmed like a candid documentary. At first the song is only heard — a faint doira behind the wall, silhouettes behind frosted glass; when the door opens the sound turns clear, and on the snare roll the street comes alive.

DOCUMENTARY LOOK: handheld cameras hidden among the people catch moments as they happen. Nobody looks into the lens or shows anything to the camera. People stand at random spots, overlap and talk over each other; foreground heads often block part of the shot. Every shot is a glimpse of one continuous real-time event. The boys sing to each other as much as to anyone; the people around keep talking among themselves. Once outside the mood turns into a warm New-Year-like street celebration and the street fills up shot by shot: people pass close in front of the lens, neighbours greet and congratulate each other, others come out of their houses.

HARD CUTS: exactly 13 shots, cutting only at the marked times on the beat of @audio1; each shot a new angle. No dissolves.

SILENCE 10.0–12.3s: nobody speaks or sings; the refusal is a quiet gesture.
RULES: only the five boys sing the lyrics from @audio1; everyone else just talks, nobody inside the house sings. No money anywhere in frame; the father's hand only moves toward his pocket and is stopped. The boys hold their yellow cap doiras up at shoulder height and strike them with their fingers; no drum between the knees. No hugging or touching between men and women; nobody clinks bottles.
